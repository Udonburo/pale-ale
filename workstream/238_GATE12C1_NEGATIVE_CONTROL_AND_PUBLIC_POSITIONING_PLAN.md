## 1. Main Positioning

Gate12C-1 is not a failed project. It is a successful negative control.

The correct public interpretation is:

- Gate12A and Gate12B found structured closure and observer-relative signatures in existing audit artifacts.
- Gate12C-0 showed that an equal-rank associator surface was feasible over the canonical real-artifact grid.
- Gate12C-1 then tested a stricter higher-order parenthesization hypothesis under a predeclared hierarchy.
- Under the matched spectrum-preserving orientation null, the Gate12C-1 effect did not exceed null.
- Therefore the system did not manufacture a positive higher-order claim.

This strengthens the audit program. The pipeline can preserve a positive Gate12B line while rejecting an attractive overextension when the stricter null-controlled test does not support it.

The public posture should be:

```text
Gate12C-1 is a null-controlled negative result that prevents overclaiming.
```

It should not be framed as a failed search for a physical analogy, a failed interpretability result, or a reason to erase Gate12A/Gate12B.

## 5. Relationship to Gate12B

Gate12B remains the active positive evidence line.

Gate12C-1 does not invalidate Gate12B because it tests a stricter and different hypothesis:

- Gate12B: observer-relative closure signatures.
- Gate12C-1: compressed-overlap parenthesization excess above matched orientation null.

The negative Gate12C-1 result narrows the theory rather than erasing Gate12A/Gate12B.

The right relationship is:

```text
Gate12B supports a bounded observer-relative closure-signature claim; Gate12C-1 blocks a stronger higher-order associator-excess claim under the frozen equal-rank null-controlled protocol.
```

Do not use Gate12C-1 as a post-hoc explanation of Gate12B. Do not use Gate12B to rescue Gate12C-1. The two lines should be connected only through an explicit manuscript boundary or a later predeclared descriptive overlay plan.
