\begin{abstract}
Gate12C-1 is a predeclared higher-order negative control over existing Gate12A
replay artifacts. It tests whether compressed-overlap parenthesization
sensitivity exceeds a matched spectrum-preserving orientation null under the
frozen equal-rank protocol. The canonical twelve-source-run surface completed
with execution status \code{complete} and grid outcome
\code{no_directional_support}: all 24 run/q endpoints were informative and
coverage-complete, and all \code{q_support} and \code{run_support} calls were
false. The result closes the associator-excess hypothesis under this frozen
protocol. It is not positive evidence for Gate12B, not a physical-structure
discovery, not a model-quality result, and not a correctness classifier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Artifact-level structural audits sit between raw transcript inspection and
final-output scalar evaluation. They preserve local objects, transport
relations, and triangle-level records that scalar scores can erase. That extra
structure is useful only if the audit pipeline can also reject attractive
extensions when a predeclared test does not support them.

Gate12C-1 records such a rejection. It was designed as a stricter higher-order
probe after the Gate12B artifact-audit line. The test asks whether real Gate12A
artifact cycles are more parenthesization-sensitive than structurally matched
orientation-randomized cycles. Under the frozen equal-rank protocol, the answer
was no.

The contribution is therefore narrow and methodological. Gate12C-1 records a
completed null-controlled negative result. It supports claim discipline: the
same audit program that can report bounded structural signals can also decline a
stronger associator-excess claim when the matched-null test does not support it.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Gate12A Source Artifacts}

Gate12C-1 operates on already materialized Gate12A replay artifacts. It does not
rerun model inference, modify model weights, change source text, or create a new
model-evaluation benchmark. The relevant source surface consists of local
objects, directed transport relations, triangle cycles, and derived metadata
already present in the Gate12A line.

\subsection{Gate12B and the Positive Paper Line}

Gate12B remains a separate bounded positive artifact-level result. It reports an
archive-family observer-relative closure-signature pattern in existing replay
artifacts. Gate12C-1 does not add empirical support to Gate12B, does not expand
Gate12B's empirical scope, and does not invalidate the Gate12B
closure-signature claim.

\subsection{Gate12C-0 and Gate12C-1}

Gate12C-0 established that an equal-rank associator surface was operationally
feasible over the canonical real-artifact grid. Gate12C-1 then tested the
stronger question: whether compressed-overlap parenthesization sensitivity
exceeded a matched spectrum-preserving orientation null under a predeclared
hierarchy.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Compressed-Overlap Parenthesization Probe}

The Gate12C-1 probe reads compressed-overlap transports derived from the
existing artifact surface. It compares parenthesization-sensitive compositions
of local transport objects and asks whether the observed artifact surface
exceeds a matched null under the frozen support rule.

This note keeps the method operational. It does not introduce new equations or
theory vocabulary beyond the tracked protocol records. The authoritative
protocol and result records are listed in Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}.

\subsection{Equal-Rank Surface}

The alpha protocol uses an equal-rank construction. This matters for the claim
boundary: the result is not a rectangular rank-mismatch result, not a statement
about all possible local-object constructions, and not a statement about every
possible null family.

\subsection{Matched Orientation Null}

The null preserves each edge's singular spectrum while randomizing orientation.
The primary question is directional: do the real artifact cycles exceed this
matched orientation-randomized, edge-spectrum-preserving null? Positive
associator magnitude alone is not evidence under this protocol.

\subsection{Hierarchy and Support Rule}

The frozen hierarchy reports endpoint-level and run-level support calls. The
primary grid outcome is determined by those calls after coverage and
multiplicity handling. In the recorded execution, every endpoint was informative
and coverage-complete, and all support calls were false.

\section{Interpretation}
\label{sec:interpretation}

Gate12C-1 prevents a stronger associator-excess hypothesis from being reported
as evidence when the predeclared matched-null test did not support it. The
result closes the specific directional-excess reading tested here. It does not
close every possible associator construction, null choice, local-object
construction, model family, or future prospective suppression/coherence
hypothesis.

This is also the connection to Gate12B. Gate12C-1 is not support for Gate12B and
not an invalidation of Gate12B. It is a claim-boundary result: a stricter,
different hypothesis was tested, completed, and returned negative under its
frozen null-controlled rule.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

The main limitations are intentionally narrow:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Current source-run surface only.} The result is bounded to the
    canonical twelve Gate12A source-run surface used by the frozen plan.
    \item \textbf{One null family.} The primary null is the matched
    spectrum-preserving orientation null. Other nulls require separate
    predeclared plans.
    \item \textbf{Equal-rank protocol only.} The result does not report a
    rectangular rank-mismatch associator.
    \item \textbf{No Gate12B overlay.} No Gate12B overlay is consumed here. Any
    overlay would be descriptive-only and would require a separate plan.
    \item \textbf{No model evaluation claim.} The result is not a model-quality
    ranking, safety or deployment guarantee, answer-quality label, or
    correctness classifier.
    \item \textbf{No weight-level mechanism.} The note makes no causal claim
    about model weights or internal mechanisms.
\end{itemize}

\section{Relation to Gate12B}
\label{sec:relation-gate12b}

Gate12B and Gate12C-1 test different hypotheses. Gate12B reports a bounded
observer-relative closure-signature result over existing artifacts. Gate12C-1
tests a stricter higher-order compressed-overlap parenthesization-excess
hypothesis above a matched orientation null.

The negative Gate12C-1 result narrows claim discipline around the Gate12B paper
line, but it does not transfer evidence to Gate12B. It also does not erase the
Gate12B result. The two lines should remain connected through explicit boundary
language or through a later predeclared descriptive compatibility plan, not
through post-hoc evidence transfer.

\section{Claim Boundary}
\label{sec:claim-boundary}

The claim ceiling is compact. Gate12C-1 reports a completed predeclared
negative control with \code{no_directional_support}. It does not report a
physical-structure discovery, a safety or deployment guarantee, a model-quality
ranking, a correctness classification rule, a weight-level causal mechanism,
Gate12B overlay evidence, or a rectangular rank-mismatch result.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Gate12C-1 closes the associator-excess hypothesis as negative under the frozen
equal-rank protocol. The completed result is \code{no_directional_support}, with

empirical work requires new predeclared plans.
