\begin{abstract}
Gate12C-1 is a higher-order negative control over existing Gate12A replay
artifacts whose protocol and support rule were predeclared before its execution
and result inspection. The test asks whether compressed-overlap
parenthesization sensitivity on the canonical twelve-source-run surface exceeds
a matched spectrum-preserving orientation null under a frozen equal-rank
protocol. The execution completed with status \code{complete} and grid outcome
\breakcode{no_directional_support}: all 24 case/q endpoints were informative
and coverage-complete, and all \breakcode{q_support} and
\breakcode{run_support} calls were
false. Under this protocol, the run provides no support for the tested
associator-excess hypothesis. The result is a claim-boundary result, not
evidence transfer to Gate12B and not a model-evaluation or physical-structure
claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Artifact-level structural audits preserve information that final-output scalar
evaluations usually discard: local objects, transport relations, cycle records,
and metadata about how those objects compose. That additional structure is
useful only if the audit program can reject attractive extensions as well as
report bounded positive signals. Without that discipline, richer telemetry can
become a source of unchecked interpretive claims.

Predeclared negative controls are one guard against that drift. In a
high-dimensional artifact audit, a surface containing graphs, cycles,
transports, and compression choices can support many plausible higher-order
probes. Without a predeclared null, endpoint family, and support rule,
positive-looking slices can be promoted while negative outcomes are treated as
incidental. Gate12C-1 fixes those degrees of freedom before reading the result,
is allowed to return negative, and did return negative.

Gate12C-1 is included as a negative control for that audit posture. It was
designed after the Gate12B audit line as a stricter higher-order probe over
existing Gate12A replay artifacts. The test asks whether real artifact cycles
show more compressed-overlap parenthesization sensitivity than a matched
orientation-randomized null that preserves edge spectra. Under the frozen
equal-rank hierarchy, no case/q endpoint met the predeclared support rule. In
this note, associator excess means
excess parenthesization sensitivity relative to that matched orientation null,
not nonzero parenthesization sensitivity by itself.

The contribution is narrow and methodological. Gate12C-1 records a completed
null-controlled negative result and fixes the corresponding claim boundary. It
shows that this audit stack can decline a stronger associator-excess
interpretation when the predeclared matched-null test does not support it.

\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Gate12A Source Surface}

Gate12C-1 operates on already materialized Gate12A replay artifacts. It does
not rerun model inference, modify model weights, change source text, or create
a new model-evaluation benchmark. The relevant surface consists of local
objects, directed transport records, triangle cycles, and derived metadata from
the Gate12A artifact line.

\subsection{Gate12B as a Separate Positive Line}

Within the tracked Gate12 project record, Gate12B is treated as a separate
bounded archive-family observer-relative closure-signature line. This note does
not re-evaluate that claim. It keeps the Gate12B and Gate12C-1 claims separate:
Gate12C-1 does not add empirical support to Gate12B, does not expand Gate12B's

\subsection{Gate12C-0 Feasibility and Gate12C-1 Test}

Gate12C-0 established that an equal-rank associator surface was operationally
feasible over the canonical real-artifact grid. Feasible here means that the
probe could be defined and evaluated on the existing artifacts; it does not
mean that Gate12C-1 excess was expected or supported. Gate12C-1 then tested a
stronger question: whether compressed-overlap parenthesization sensitivity
exceeded a matched spectrum-preserving orientation null under a predeclared
support hierarchy.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Gate12C-1 is best read as an operational negative-control test, not as a new
theory layer. The protocol fixes the source surface, the rank regime, the null
family, and the support hierarchy before interpreting the result.

The following were frozen before execution:

\begin{itemize}
    \item canonical twelve source-run surface;
    \item eligible-triangle criteria;
    \item \breakcode{equal_rank_alpha} rank regime;
    \item matched orientation-null family;
    \item orientation-null draw settings;
    \item aggregation hierarchy;
    \item 24 endpoint family;
    \item support rule.
\end{itemize}

Throughout this note, a \emph{source-run case} means one of the twelve
canonical Gate12A source runs. A \emph{root row} is one cyclic rooting of an
eligible triangle at one compression rank. A \emph{source block} is a
predeclared source-sample block identified by
\breakcode{source_sample_block_id}; a
cycle belongs to a valid single-sample block only when all three nodes resolve
to that same block. A \emph{case/q endpoint} means one predeclared endpoint for
one source-run case and one nontrivial compression rank \code{q}.

Operationally, the observed quantity is a compressed-overlap parenthesization
readout on the real artifact cycles. The comparator is a matched
orientation-null distribution: it preserves the singular spectrum on each edge
while randomizing orientation. The comparison is therefore not against zero.
For this note, \emph{associator excess} means directional excess of the
observed readout above that matched orientation-null distribution after the
frozen support hierarchy and multiplicity rules are applied.

For each ordered eligible triangle, the probe starts from three raw-overlap
operators along the triangle edges. One compressed branch first composes and
compresses one adjacent pair before applying the remaining edge; the other
branch first composes and compresses the other adjacent pair. The readout
compares the resulting compressed operators. This is the operational
parenthesization-sensitive quantity tested against the matched null.

The following notation is runner notation for the operational readout, not new
theory. All edge operators are real square matrices on the common equal-rank
space. For an ordered eligible triangle
\[
v\sb{0}\rightarrow v\sb{1}\rightarrow v\sb{2}\rightarrow v\sb{0},
\]
let
\[
M\sb{0}=C(v\sb{1}\leftarrow v\sb{0}),\quad
M\sb{1}=C(v\sb{2}\leftarrow v\sb{1}),\quad
M\sb{2}=C(v\sb{0}\leftarrow v\sb{2})
\]
denote the three raw-overlap operators on the directed edges. The operator
\(C(v\sb j\leftarrow v\sb i)\) maps column coordinates at \(v\sb i\) to
column coordinates at \(v\sb j\). Matrices act on column vectors from the
left, and products compose right-to-left; therefore
\(M\sb 2M\sb 1M\sb 0\) maps the \(v\sb 0\) space around the ordered cycle
back to itself.

If \(X=U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}\), singular values are ordered
nonincreasingly. Let \(U\sb q\) and \(V\sb q\) contain the first \(q\)
singular-vector columns and let \(\Sigma\sb q\) contain the retained singular
values. The frozen compression operator returns the reconstructed rank-\(q\)
matrix
\[
Q\sb q(X)=U\sb q\Sigma\sb q V\sb q^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The same construction is applied to observed and null products. For the
ordered singular values of \(X\), the frozen split gap is
\[
g\sb q(X)=
\frac{\sigma\sb q(X)-\sigma\sb{q+1}(X)}
{\max\{\sigma\sb 1(X),\epsilon\}},
\qquad \epsilon=10^{-12}.
\]
Both intermediate two-edge products \(M\sb 2M\sb 1\) and
\(M\sb 1M\sb 0\) must satisfy
\(g\sb q>\tau\sb{\mathrm{split}}\), with frozen
\(\tau\sb{\mathrm{split}}=10^{-3}\). The same threshold applies to every
null product. A near-degenerate cut is not forced through an arbitrary
singular-vector basis. The two parenthesized branches are
\[
L\sb{q}=Q\sb{q}(M\sb{2}M\sb{1})M\sb{0},\quad
R\sb{q}=M\sb{2}Q\sb{q}(M\sb{1}M\sb{0}),
\]
and the observed readout is
\[
a\sb{q}=\lVert L\sb{q}-R\sb{q}\rVert\sb{F},
\]
where \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\sb F\) is the standard Frobenius norm.
These displayed terms correspond to runner fields \code{M0}, \code{M1},
\code{M2}, \code{Q_q}, \code{L_q}, \code{R_q}, and \code{a_q}.

\subsection{Eligible Triangle and Compressed-Overlap Readout}

An eligible triangle is a Gate12A-defined residual-bearing explicit triangle
with \breakcode{holonomy_status} equal to \code{defined}, all three legs marked
\breakcode{equal_rank_orthogonal}, one common positive node rank \(r\geq 2\), and
stable left and right cuts at the selected \(q\). The canonical surface has
\(r=3\), tests \(q\in\{1,2\}\), and evaluates all three cyclic roots. If an
observed root fails either cut, that root is aggregation-ineligible; because a
valid cycle/q requires exactly three valid roots, the cycle/q is then excluded
and counted in coverage diagnostics. A failed cut on a null product invalidates
that draw for the affected row and invokes the frozen retry accounting.

For each source-run case, the expected cycle denominator is the distinct set of
preflight-eligible cycles frozen by the Gate12C-0 census. The expected block
denominator is the distinct set of \breakcode{single_sample}
\breakcode{source_sample_block_id} values represented by those cycles before
primary filtering. Repeated block identifiers are aggregated as one block; a
block is represented at \(q\) when it contains at least one valid cycle/q. A
missing, mixed, or undefined block assignment forces coverage failure, as does
a zero expected-block denominator. No row, cycle, or block is imputed.

\subsection{Matched Orientation Null}

The null preserves each edge's singular spectrum while randomizing the relative
orientations used by the triangle composition. This makes the comparison a
matched orientation test rather than a comparison against zero or arbitrary
random matrices. The null is not a gauge transform and is not required to be
realizable by one globally consistent set of node bases. Under this protocol,
positive associator magnitude is not evidence by itself; support requires
directional excess relative to the matched null after the frozen hierarchy is
applied.

For each cycle and null draw, the runner generates one randomized triple of
edge operators and reuses that triple across all three cyclic roots and both
tested compression ranks. For an observed edge spectrum \(\Sigma\sb e\), the
null edge has the form
\[
\widetilde C\sb e^{(b)}=
O\sb{e,\mathrm L}^{(b)}\Sigma\sb e
O\sb{e,\mathrm R}^{(b)\mathsf T}.
\]
The two orientation matrices are separately seeded from a stable SHA256 hash of
the frozen null seed, cycle identifier, edge identifier, draw index, and
orientation-side label. Hash-derived standard-normal matrices are reduced by QR
decomposition and column-sign normalization to obtain the deterministic
orthogonal orientations. Null accounting records valid, invalid-cut, and total
attempt counts; retries continue until 255 valid draws are obtained or the
frozen maximum of 1024 attempts is reached. Draws with a near-degenerate
compression cut are marked invalid rather than assigned an arbitrary SVD basis.

\subsection{Support Rule}

The frozen hierarchy produces endpoint-level and source-run-case support calls.
For an observed root-row readout \(a\sb{q,\mathrm{obs}}\), valid null-draw
readouts \(a\sb q^{(b)}\), and frozen \(\epsilon=10^{-12}\), the primary row
effect is
\[
d\sb q=\log\!\left(
\frac{a\sb{q,\mathrm{obs}}+\epsilon}
{\operatorname{median}\sb b\{a\sb q^{(b)}\}+\epsilon}
\right).
\]
This row effect and the row-level null summaries are telemetry; they do not by
themselves assign support or discovery claims.

For each \code{q}, the hierarchy takes the median \(d\sb q\) across all three
valid cyclic roots to obtain a cycle/q value, the median across valid cycles in
one source block to obtain a source-block/q score, and the median across source
blocks to obtain the case/q median. The source-block scores, rather than
overlapping root or cycle rows, are the inputs to the one-sided exact sign test.
Scores above \(10^{-12}\) are positive, scores below \(-10^{-12}\) are
negative, and values within that tolerance are ties excluded from the sign-test
sample size. Mixed or undefined source blocks fail coverage; there is no
imputation. Coverage requires at least 90 percent of preflight-eligible cycles
to have all three valid roots and at least 90 percent of expected source blocks
to be represented.

If \(s\) is the positive-block count and \(n\) is the positive-plus-negative
block count, the one-sided raw value is
\[
p\sb{\mathrm{raw}}=\sum\sb{k=s}^{n}\binom{n}{k}2^{-n}.
\]
When \(n=0\), the endpoint is non-informative and receives
\(p\sb{\mathrm{raw}}=1\).

This exact-binomial calculation is the frozen endpoint decision calibration.
It is not interpreted as population-level inference; that interpretation would
additionally require independence or exchangeability across source blocks,
which is not claimed here.

Holm correction is then applied across all 24 case/q endpoints. A case/q
endpoint receives \breakcode{q_support} only when coverage passes, the sign test is
informative, the case/q median exceeds the frozen zero tolerance, and the
Holm-adjusted one-sided value is below 0.05. Coverage-failed and non-informative
endpoints remain in that family with raw value 1. A source-run case receives
\breakcode{run_support} only when both tested q endpoints satisfy that rule. In the
recorded execution, no endpoint failed coverage and no support call was
positive.

\section{Interpretation}
\label{sec:interpretation}

Gate12C-1 provides no support for one specific support criterion: excess
compressed-overlap associator signal above the matched orientation null under
the frozen equal-rank protocol. It prevents that stronger higher-order
interpretation from being reported as a positive result.

The result does not establish a general impossibility theorem. It does not rule
out other predeclared null families, rectangular rank-mismatch associators,
alternative local-object constructions, other model families, or other future
prospective hypotheses. Those would require new plans before they could support
new claims.

This distinction is the main interpretive boundary. Gate12C-1 is negative under
its own protocol. It is not support for Gate12B, not a post-hoc explanation of
Gate12B, and not an invalidation of Gate12B.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

The limitations are mostly consequences of the predeclared design:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Current source-run surface only.} The result is bounded to the
    canonical twelve Gate12A source runs used by the frozen plan.
    \item \textbf{Block-aware decision calibration.} Source-sample blocks are
    the predeclared sign-test units so that overlapping root and cycle rows are
    not counted as separate observations. The design does not establish
    independence across source blocks or authorize population-level inference;
    dependence among blocks would limit the inferential reading of the exact
    binomial calibration beyond this frozen decision rule.
    \item \textbf{One null family.} The primary null tests excess relative to
    orientation-randomized cycles with preserved edge spectra. It is not a test
    against every possible structural randomization, and other nulls require
    separate predeclared plans.
    \item \textbf{Equal-rank protocol only.} The result does not report a
    rectangular rank-mismatch associator.
    \item \textbf{No Gate12B overlay.} No Gate12B overlay is consumed here. A
    later overlay could only be descriptive compatibility analysis under a
    separate plan.
    \item \textbf{No model evaluation claim.} The result is not a model-quality
    ranking, safety or deployment guarantee, answer-quality label, or
    correctness classifier.
    \item \textbf{No weight-level mechanism.} The note makes no causal claim
    about model weights or internal mechanisms.
\end{itemize}

These limitations are part of the evidentiary boundary rather than omissions to
repair inside this note. The negative call is interpretable because it is tied
to a fixed source surface, fixed null, fixed rank regime, and fixed support
rule.

\section{Relation to Gate12B}
\label{sec:relation-gate12b}

Gate12B and Gate12C-1 address different questions. Gate12B reports a bounded
observer-relative closure-signature result over existing artifacts. Gate12C-1
tests a stricter compressed-overlap parenthesization-excess hypothesis above a
matched orientation null.

The relationship is claim discipline, not evidence transfer. Gate12B remains
the bounded positive artifact-level line. Gate12C-1 rejects the support
criterion for a stronger higher-order associator-excess claim under its own
frozen protocol. The two lines may be discussed together only to show that the
audit program can hold both a bounded positive result and a predeclared
negative control without turning the latter into support for the former.

\section{Claim Boundary}
\label{sec:claim-boundary}

The claim ceiling is compact. Gate12C-1 reports a completed predeclared
negative control with \breakcode{no_directional_support}. It does not report a
physical-structure discovery, a safety or deployment guarantee, a model-quality
ranking, a correctness classification rule, a weight-level causal mechanism,
Gate12B overlay evidence, or a rectangular rank-mismatch result.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Gate12C-1 provides no support for associator excess under the frozen equal-rank
protocol. The completed result is \breakcode{no_directional_support}, with all 24
case/q endpoints informative and coverage-complete and all support calls false.
Its value is in claim discipline: the audit stack can reject a stronger
higher-order extension rather than automatically convert it into a positive
claim. Future extensions of the null family, rank regime, source surface, or
Gate12B overlay require separate predeclared plans.
