\title{Compression-Interleaved Parenthesization Defects\\
in LLM Replay Artifact Graphs:\\
A Predeclared Null Test}
\author{Aoi Kawasaki}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Low-rank compression can induce parenthesization sensitivity even though
ordinary matrix multiplication is associative. We test whether this
compression-interleaved defect is larger in LLM replay-artifact cycles than
under an edge-spectrum-matched, graph-unconstrained orientation null. The
source surface, eligible-cycle criteria, compression ranks, null procedure,
aggregation hierarchy, and one-sided support rule were fixed before execution.
Across twelve source runs and two nontrivial compression ranks, all 24
endpoints were informative and coverage-complete, and none met the predeclared
criterion for directional excess. Descriptively, all 24 endpoint median
log-ratios were negative; this reverse-direction regularity was not predeclared
and is not treated as confirmatory evidence. Because the null preserves
marginal edge spectra while breaking shared-node realizability, the test cannot
determine whether the negative comparison reflects a genuinely smaller
observed defect or inflation of the null caused by destroying shared-node
coupling. The result therefore does not support the targeted excess claim under
the frozen protocol and motivates a separate prospective test using
graph-constrained nulls, held-out runs, and positive controls.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Ordinary matrix multiplication is associative, but an approximation operator
inserted between multiplications need not respect that identity. If \(\Q_q\)
denotes rank-\(q\) truncated singular-value reconstruction, then
\(\Q_q(M_2M_1)M_0\) and \(M_2\Q_q(M_1M_0)\) can differ even though the
uncompressed products are equal. The difference is a parenthesization defect
created by the placement of compression, not a failure of matrix
associativity.

Replay artifact graphs provide a concrete setting in which to measure this
defect. Their nodes carry local low-dimensional representation frames, their
directed edges carry overlap operators between frames, and their cycles expose
whether locally composed transports agree around a shared graph structure. A
larger defect on observed cycles than under the edge-spectrum-matched,
graph-unconstrained orientation null would indicate
that the frozen observable detects directional excess beyond marginal edge
spectra. The present question is deliberately narrower than whether any defect
is nonzero.

We report a predeclared test of that question over twelve source runs and two
nontrivial compression ranks. Its three main empirical results are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item all 24 endpoints were informative and coverage-complete;
    \item none of the 24 endpoints met the predeclared support rule for
    directional excess over the null; and
    \item all 24 endpoint median log-ratios were negative, a complete-family
    direction reported descriptively because a reverse-direction hypothesis
    was not predeclared.
\end{enumerate}

We refer to the frozen protocol internally as Gate12C-1. It reads the artifact
surface created by the Gate12A structural-replay line and is distinct from the
Gate12B observer-relative closure audit. That project history supplies the data
and provenance, but the scientific subject of this paper is the
compression-interleaved parenthesization test itself.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\textbf{Low-rank approximation and truncation order.}
Truncated singular-value reconstruction is the optimal fixed-rank matrix
approximation in Frobenius norm under the classical Eckart--Young result
\cite{eckart1936approximation}. That pointwise optimality does not imply that
inserting truncation inside a product preserves associativity. More broadly,
sequentially truncated multilinear decompositions treat the sequence of
approximation operations as part of the algorithm and can differ from a single
uninterleaved decomposition \cite{vannieuwenhoven2012truncation}. The present
observable isolates the analogous order sensitivity for two specified
intermediate matrix products.

\textbf{Procrustes and polar transport.}
Orthogonal Procrustes alignment uses an SVD-derived orthogonal map to align two
coordinate systems \cite{schonemann1966procrustes}; the orthogonal polar factor
provides the closely related nearest-orthogonal construction
\cite{higham1986polar}. These methods motivate treating overlap-derived
orthogonal or partial-isometry maps as transports between local representation
frames. Here the tested matrices are the frozen raw overlap operators, and the
rank truncation acts on their two-edge products.

\textbf{Discrete connections, gauges, and representation holonomy.}
Connection-Laplacian methods place orthogonal transformations on graph edges
and use their composition to analyze high-dimensional data
\cite{singer2012vector}. Gauge-equivariant neural networks formalize the demand
that computations be independent of local frame choices
\cite{cohen2019gauge}. Recent work on neural representation holonomy likewise
composes Procrustes-based local alignments around loops and constructs a
gauge-invariant statistic \cite{sevetlidis2026holonomy}. Our graph, source
artifacts, null, and parenthesization observable are different, but the shared
mathematical requirement is that a reported loop quantity not depend on
arbitrary node-wise orthogonal bases.

Javidnia develops a discrete gauge-theoretic and sheaf-theoretic atlas of LLM
representations, including spanning-tree gauge fixing and fundamental-cycle
holonomy \cite{javidnia2026superposition}. Our setting differs in both
observational substrate and target quantity: we operate on frozen replay
artifact graphs rather than context-chart representations, and test
compression-interleaved parenthesization defects against a predeclared
edge-spectrum-matched, graph-unconstrained orientation null rather than
estimating representational holonomy.

\textbf{Pale-ale artifact studies.}
The source surface descends from Structural Replay under a frozen FP32 regime
\cite{kawasaki2026structural}. The transport-first note defines
gauge-covariant closure telemetry on replay artifact graphs
\cite{kawasaki2026telemetry}, and the Gate12B report studies bounded
observer-relative closure signatures \cite{kawasaki2026observer}. The current
paper neither re-tests nor extends those empirical claims. It asks a separate,
predeclared question about compression-interleaved defects relative to a
specified null.

\section{Formal Setup}
\label{sec:formal-setup}

Let \(\mathcal{G}=(V,E)\) be a replay artifact graph. Each node \(v\) carries
an orthonormal local frame \(U_v\) of common active rank \(r\). A directed edge
from \(v_i\) to \(v_j\) carries the raw overlap operator
\[
C(v_j\leftarrow v_i)=U_{v_j}^{\mathsf T}U_{v_i},
\]
which maps coordinates at \(v_i\) to coordinates at \(v_j\). Matrices act on
column vectors from the left, and products compose right-to-left.

For an ordered eligible triangle
\[
v_0\longrightarrow v_1\longrightarrow v_2\longrightarrow v_0,
\]
write
\[
M_0=C(v_1\leftarrow v_0),\qquad
M_1=C(v_2\leftarrow v_1),\qquad
M_2=C(v_0\leftarrow v_2).
\]
The uncompressed cycle operator \(M_2M_1M_0\) maps the \(v_0\) coordinate
space to itself.

If \(X=U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}\) has singular values in nonincreasing order,
define the rank-\(q\) truncated reconstruction
\[
\Q_q(X)=U_q\Sigma_qV_q^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For \(\epsilon=10^{-12}\), the boundary-gap diagnostic is
\[
g_q(X)=
\frac{\sigma_q(X)-\sigma_{q+1}(X)}
{\max\{\sigma_1(X),\epsilon\}}.
\]
The frozen protocol requires \(g_q>10^{-3}\) for both intermediate products,
so the selected rank-\(q\) approximation is separated at its truncation
boundary.

The two compression-interleaved parenthesizations are
\[
L_q=\Q_q(M_2M_1)M_0,
\qquad
R_q=M_2\Q_q(M_1M_0),
\]
and the scalar defect is
\[
a_q=\lVert L_q-R_q\rVert_F.
\]
\emph{Associator} is used operationally for the defect induced by the frozen
intermediate-compression rule; ordinary matrix multiplication itself remains
associative.

\begin{proposition}[Gauge invariance]
\label{prop:gauge-invariance}
Assume
\[
\sigma_q(X)>\sigma_{q+1}(X)
\qquad\text{for }X\in\{M_2M_1,M_1M_0\}.
\]
Then the scalar defect is invariant under independent node-wise orthogonal
changes of basis. In particular, if \(G_i\in O(r)\) and
\[
C(v_j\leftarrow v_i)'=G_j^{\mathsf T}
C(v_j\leftarrow v_i)G_i,
\]
then \(a_q'=a_q\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The strict boundary separation makes the rank-\(q\) reconstructed matrix
unique, although a particular choice of singular-vector basis within the
retained or discarded subspace need not be unique. The reconstructed matrix
therefore obeys the orthogonal covariance identity
\[
\Q_q(S^{\mathsf T}XT)=S^{\mathsf T}\Q_q(X)T
\]
for orthogonal \(S,T\). For the ordered triangle,
\[
M_0'=G_1^{\mathsf T}M_0G_0,\quad
M_1'=G_2^{\mathsf T}M_1G_1,\quad
M_2'=G_0^{\mathsf T}M_2G_2.
\]
Cancellation of adjacent orthogonal factors and covariance of \(\Q_q\) give
\[
L_q'=G_0^{\mathsf T}L_qG_0,
\qquad
R_q'=G_0^{\mathsf T}R_qG_0.
\]
Thus \(L_q'-R_q'=G_0^{\mathsf T}(L_q-R_q)G_0\). Orthogonal invariance of the
Frobenius norm yields \(a_q'=a_q\).
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge-invariance} makes \(a_q\) a well-defined scalar on
the artifact graph rather than a diagnostic tied to one stored choice of local
bases. Because the split-gap diagnostic depends only on singular values,
eligibility is also invariant under node-wise orthogonal gauge changes. The
edge-spectrum-matched, graph-unconstrained orientation null is defined from
edge spectra, so the full observed-to-null comparison is gauge-independent
under the frozen protocol. The runner's deterministic gauge control evaluates
a signed-permutation subset of these changes; Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge-invariance}
states the corresponding mathematical result for the full orthogonal class at
strictly separated cuts.

\section{Predeclared Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

The source surface comprised twelve already materialized replay-artifact runs:
four model lines crossed with transcript, briefing, and archive rendering
families. The three rendering families are distinct artifact constructions over
the frozen source benchmark rather than independent task datasets.
Table~\ref{tab:case-map} gives the case map. Each eligible triangle was
residual-bearing, had defined Gate12A holonomy, had three
equal-rank-orthogonal legs with common rank \(r=3\), belonged to a valid
single-sample source block, and had stable intermediate cuts. The two
nontrivial ranks were \(q\in\{1,2\}\), evaluated at all three cyclic roots.

The comparator was fixed as an \emph{edge-spectrum-matched,
graph-unconstrained orientation null}. For each observed edge spectrum
\(\Sigma_e\), null draw \(b\) used
\[
\widetilde C_e^{(b)}=
O_{e,\mathrm L}^{(b)}\Sigma_e
O_{e,\mathrm R}^{(b)\mathsf T},
\]
with independently generated orthogonal left and right orientations. One
randomized edge triple was reused across the three roots and both values of
\(q\) within a cycle draw. The procedure requested 255 valid draws, allowed at
most 1024 attempts when stable-cut requirements failed, and applied the same
\(10^{-3}\) split-gap threshold to observed and null products. The null
preserves every edge's singular values but is not required to arise from a
single consistent choice of bases at shared nodes.

For one observed root and its valid null draws, the primary log-ratio was
\[
d_q=\log\left(
\frac{a_{q,\mathrm{obs}}+\epsilon}
{\operatorname{median}_b a_q^{(b)}+\epsilon}
\right).
\]
The frozen hierarchy took medians over three roots to obtain a cycle/\(q\)
value, over valid cycles within each source-sample block to obtain a block
score, and over represented blocks to obtain an endpoint median. Coverage
required at least 90\% of expected cycles and 90\% of expected blocks, with no
imputation.

The confirmatory test was one-sided in the positive direction. Block scores
above \(10^{-12}\) were positive, scores below \(-10^{-12}\) were negative,
and intervening values were ties. If \(s\) of \(n\) non-tied block scores were
positive, the frozen exact-binomial calibration was
\[
p_{\mathrm{raw}}=\sum_{k=s}^{n}\binom{n}{k}2^{-n}.
\]
Holm correction was applied across all 24 case/\(q\) endpoints
\cite{holm1979simple}. Endpoint support required complete coverage, an
informative sign test, a positive endpoint median, and Holm-adjusted
\(p<0.05\). This calibration is the predeclared decision rule; it is not a
claim that source blocks form an independent population sample.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

None of the 24 predeclared endpoints supported directional excess. All
endpoints were informative and coverage-complete. In addition, every endpoint
median log-ratio was negative. The latter complete-family direction was not a
predeclared reverse-direction hypothesis and is reported descriptively only.

A post-hoc descriptive reporting addendum was committed before computing any
new aggregate (commit \code{671e6d1}). Under that boundary, exponentiating the
24 endpoint-level hierarchical summaries gives values from 0.028 to 0.236,
with a median of 0.066 on that summary scale. These are transformed endpoint
summaries, not raw-row median ratios or a reverse-direction test.

The confirmatory conclusion follows from the frozen positive-direction rule:
0/24 endpoints supported excess over the edge-spectrum-matched,
graph-unconstrained orientation null. This was not a coverage-limited outcome;
all 24 endpoints represented every expected cycle and source block and had an
informative sign test. Appendix~\ref{app:p-values} gives the recorded raw and
Holm-adjusted values.

The descriptive conclusion is separate. The endpoint family itself was fixed
before execution, so its full direction pattern can be reported without
selecting a favorable subgroup. Nevertheless, the hypothesis that all or most
endpoints would be negative was not predeclared, and no reverse-direction
test, subgroup analysis, or additional significance claim is introduced here.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the predeclared test rules out}

The result blocks the targeted claim that replay-artifact cycles show positive
compression-interleaved defect excess over this null under the frozen ranks,
coverage criteria, aggregation hierarchy, and support rule. It does not show
that the observed defect is identically zero, and it does not establish a
general absence of graph structure.

Gate12B addresses a different question: a bounded observer-relative closure
signature on an existing artifact surface. Gate12C-1 supplies neither positive
evidence for that result nor a refutation of it. The connection is procedural:
the same research program can report a bounded positive artifact result while
declining a stronger, unsupported excess claim.

\subsection{Descriptive reverse-direction regularity}

All 24 endpoint medians were negative, spanning every model line, rendering
family, and tested rank in the predeclared grid. This is a conspicuous
complete-family pattern and belongs in the result record. It remains
descriptive because the protocol did not predeclare a reverse-direction
hypothesis or a decision rule for family-wide negativity. We therefore do not
label it confirmatory evidence, a suppression effect, or a discovery of graph
coherence.

\subsection{What the current null cannot distinguish}

The null matches each edge's singular spectrum but samples its left and right
orientations independently. At a shared node, adjacent null edges therefore
need not use one common latent basis. The null can generate edge triples that
are not realizable by any single node-wise assignment of frames, even though
each edge is marginally spectrum-matched.

Consequently, the negative endpoint medians admit more than one explanation.
They may reflect an absence of the targeted positive excess, an increase in
null defects caused by destroying shared-node coupling, or a combination of
the two. The present design does not identify which explanation is operative.
It therefore supports the observed directional comparison but not the causal
claims that real graphs are coherent or that graph consistency produced the
negative direction.

\subsection{Next prospective test}

A separate Gate12C-2 plan should predeclare the reverse-direction hypothesis
and evaluate it on held-out runs. Its null should be graph-constrained and
node-consistent, so all incident edges inherit compatible orientations from
shared node transformations. A synthetic positive control should establish
that the pipeline detects a known compression-interleaved effect, and frozen
sensitivity checks should test dependence on rank, split-gap threshold, and
null construction. None of those prospective results is imported into the
current paper.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Edge-spectrum-matched, graph-unconstrained orientation null.} Spectrum matching is edge-wise;
    shared-node realizability is not preserved. This is the central
    interpretive limitation of the comparison.
    \item \textbf{No predeclared positive control.} The execution verified
    algebraic, gauge, coverage, and deterministic-output controls, but did not
    include a synthetic instance with a known positive directional effect.
    Sensitivity to such an effect remains to be demonstrated prospectively.
    \item \textbf{Fixed source surface.} The evidence is limited to twelve
    source runs from four dense-transformer model lines and three rendering
    families. It is not a population-wide claim about LLMs.
    \item \textbf{Decision calibration.} Source-sample blocks avoid treating
    overlapping roots and cycles as separate observations, but independence or
    exchangeability across blocks is not established. The exact-binomial
    calculation is therefore read as a frozen decision calibration, not broad
    population inference.
    \item \textbf{Equal-rank construction.} The common node rank was three and
    the tested compression ranks were one and two. Rectangular rank mismatch
    and other compression rules remain outside the claim.
    \item \textbf{Artifact-level scope.} The observable is defined on replay
    artifacts. It is not a model-quality ranking, an answer-correctness
    classifier, a safety guarantee, or a weight-level causal mechanism.
\end{itemize}

file \code{gate12c1_run_q_tests.csv}, the cycle- and block-level score exports,
the complete summarizer manifest and checksums, and a software-environment
receipt. Until that capsule is posted, the tracked result memo is the public
endpoint-level record and the generated aggregation surfaces are not yet
publicly archived.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We tested whether compression-interleaved parenthesization defects in LLM
replay-artifact cycles exceeded an edge-spectrum-matched,
graph-unconstrained orientation null. All 24 predeclared endpoints were
informative and coverage-complete, and none supported the targeted positive
excess claim. Descriptively, all 24 endpoint median log-ratios were negative.
That reverse-direction regularity was not predeclared and is not confirmatory
evidence. Because the null breaks shared-node realizability, the current test
cannot identify whether graph-level coupling contributed to the direction of
the comparison. A prospective held-out test with a node-consistent null,
reverse-direction rule, positive control, and frozen sensitivity analysis is
required to answer that question.
