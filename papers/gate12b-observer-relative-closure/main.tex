\begin{abstract}
We present Gate12B, a read-only secondary audit for studying
observer-relative closure signatures in already materialized Gate12A inference
transport artifacts. A closure signature is treated here as a relation-patterned
candidate surface, not as a correctness label or model-quality score. Across
four dense-transformer model lines, archive-family Gate12B surfaces repeatedly
place high-tension candidates on \code{residual_chord=3} and flat candidates on
\code{residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2}. The signal survives recorded observer,
scale, motif observer-scope, and bounded local reparameterization sensitivities.
A selected source-facing archive queue aligns high-side R candidates with
\code{conflict-following} rows in 8/8 cases and flat-side M candidates with
\code{support-following} or \code{non-gluing} rows in 8/8 cases. Transcript and
briefing sensitivities do not reproduce the same clean archive alignment. We
present the result as a bounded artifact report, not as a universal
interpretability law, correctness classifier, model-quality benchmark, or
physical invariant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM evaluation often compresses generated output into scalar scores or
task-level labels. Those views are useful, but they discard much of the
structure present in intermediate audit artifacts. This report takes a
different stance. It studies existing replay artifacts as structured records:
local objects, directed transport relations, explicit triangle cycles, and
source-facing text surfaces are inspected without rerunning model inference and
without converting the result into a model ranking.

The artifact line used here is Gate12A/Gate12B. Gate12A emits discrete
inference transport artifacts. Gate12B reads those artifacts as a secondary
audit and asks whether relation-patterned closure signatures remain visible
when the same candidate surface is viewed through different observer scopes,
coarse-grained scale modes, and a bounded local reparameterization check.
Gate12B does not alter Gate12A semantics, thresholds, classifications, or
source text. It is an artifact reader.

The main observation is archive-family bounded. In the current
dense-transformer artifact set, archive-family Gate12B surfaces repeatedly
place high-tension candidates on \code{residual_chord=3} and flat candidates on
\code{residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2}. The same queue and source-facing
annotation path does not make transcript and briefing surfaces reproduce the
same clean archive alignment. This contrast matters because it reduces the
risk that the archive result is merely a consequence of the annotation rule or
the motif observer expansion.

This report contributes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a read-only secondary audit framing for existing inference transport
    artifacts;
    \item an observer, scale, and bounded local reparameterization sensitivity
    surface for closure signatures;
    \item a bounded archive-family relation-signature result across four
    dense-transformer model lines;
    \item source-facing queue and derived annotation evidence connecting
    selected structural candidates back to text surfaces;
    \item non-archive sensitivities showing that transcript and briefing do not
    reproduce the same clean archive alignment.
\end{enumerate}

The paper follows a deliberately conservative path.
Section~\ref{sec:artifact-regime} defines the artifact regime.
Section~\ref{sec:definitions} gives operational definitions for external
readers. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the Gate12B read-only audit and
candidate construction. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the archive-family
relation-signature flip, sensitivity checks, source-facing annotation, and
non-archive sensitivity. Section~\ref{sec:threats} states threats to validity.
Section~\ref{sec:manifest} maps claims to artifacts and integrity checks. The
conclusion restates the bounded claim and the non-claims.

\subsection{What We Claim}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bounded artifact phenomenon.} We report a repeated
    archive-family closure signature in existing Gate12A/Gate12B audit
    artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Archive-family relation signature.} Across four
    dense-transformer model lines, archive-family Gate12B surfaces repeatedly
    place high-tension candidates on \code{residual_chord=3} and flat candidates
    on \code{residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2}.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity survival.} The signal survives the recorded
    observer, scale, motif observer-scope, and basis-preserving local
    reparameterization sensitivities.
    \item \textbf{Source-facing alignment.} A selected archive source-facing
    queue aligns high-side R candidates with \code{conflict-following} rows and
    flat-side M candidates with \code{support-following} or \code{non-gluing}
    rows.
    \item \textbf{Non-archive sensitivity.} Transcript and briefing
    sensitivities do not reproduce the same clean archive alignment under the
    same queue and derived annotation path.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What We Do NOT Claim}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{No universal law.} We do not claim a universal law of LLM
    reasoning.
    \item \textbf{No benchmark ranking.} We do not present Gate12B as a
    model-quality benchmark or correctness classifier.
    \item \textbf{No answer-quality labels.} Source-facing tags describe the
    direction of a queued source row only; they are not answer-quality labels.
    \item \textbf{No weight-level claim.} We make no claim about model weights
    in general or about causal mechanisms inside a model.
    \item \textbf{No physical invariant.} The bounded reparameterization checks
    are artifact-level stability checks, not a physical invariant.
    \item \textbf{No checkpoint claim.} This report is not a checkpoint,
    release, or replacement for Gate12A.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Controlled Artifact Regime}

artifacts and emits secondary audit summaries. No model inference is run for
Gate12B. No Gate12A artifact schema, threshold, classification, or source
semantics is changed.

\section{Definitions and Terminology}
\label{sec:definitions}

This section fixes the vocabulary used in the report. The definitions are
operational. They describe how the artifact readers work; they are not
philosophical or semantic claims about reasoning in general.

\textbf{Gate12A artifact.} A Gate12A artifact is a materialized audit bundle
containing local proxy objects, directed transport relations, explicit triangle
cycle registries, closure residuals, and manifests. For source-facing
inspection, a matching Gate12A text-surface audit supplies prompt, answer,
support-anchor, and conflict-anchor fields joined to triangle-cycle rows.

\textbf{Gate12B secondary audit.} Gate12B is a read-only artifact reader over
Gate12A outputs. It builds observer-relative and coarse-grained views of
closure residuals, emits candidate rows that satisfy support requirements, and
checks a bounded local reparameterization condition when array-level transport
operators are present.

\textbf{Local object.} A local object is the artifact-level representation used
by Gate12A to attach replay state to nodes and transport relations. This report
does not interpret local objects as model-internal variables.

\textbf{Transport relation.} A transport relation is a directed artifact edge
connecting local objects in the replay graph. In the selected Gate12A surface,
relations can be summarized by relation kinds such as \code{trusted_tree} and
\code{residual_chord}.

\textbf{Trusted-tree edge.} A \code{trusted_tree} relation is an edge belonging
to the trusted-tree part of the selected Gate12A relation decomposition. It is
used as a relation kind in triangle signatures; it is not a correctness label.

\textbf{Residual-chord edge.} A \code{residual_chord} relation is a residual
edge in the selected Gate12A relation decomposition. It is a relation kind used
in cycle signatures; it is not an answer-quality label by itself.

\textbf{Triangle cycle.} A triangle cycle is an explicit three-leg cycle in the
Gate12A cycle registry. Gate12B uses these cycles as the base surface for
closure residuals and relation-signature grouping.

\textbf{Closure signature.} In this report, a closure signature is not a scalar
score and not a correctness judgment. It is a relation-patterned candidate
surface observed after applying Gate12B support requirements across observer
and scale views.

\textbf{Relation signature.} A relation signature summarizes the relation
kinds appearing on a selected triangle path. The shorthand
\code{residual_chord=3} means that all three legs in the selected triangle
signature are residual-chord relations. The shorthand
\code{residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2} means that one leg is a residual-chord
relation and two legs are trusted-tree relations. These strings should not be
overinterpreted as semantic labels.

\textbf{Candidate.} A Gate12B candidate is a cycle row that survives candidate
construction requirements. It is not simply a top-k triangle. A candidate must
have support across independent observer scopes, support across multiple scale
modes, and at least one non-triangle coarse-scale support mode.

\textbf{High-tension and flat.} High-tension and flat are residual-band sides
used by the candidate extractor. They are candidate sides, not correctness
classes. A high-tension row may be source-facing conflict-following, but that
does not make high-tension a general failure label. A flat row may be
source-facing support-following or non-gluing, but that does not make flat a
general correctness label.

\textbf{Observer.} An observer is an artifact-level view used to condition
which cycles participate in the candidate surface. Gate12B groups observer
views with identical cycle membership into independent observer scopes before
counting support.

\textbf{Scale and coarse-graining.} A scale mode groups the same triangle rows
at different levels of aggregation. The current runner includes triangle-level
scale plus relation-kind, anchor-policy, and residual-quantile scale modes.
Candidate rows must be supported across enough scale modes and must include at
least one non-triangle coarse-scale mode.

\textbf{Bounded local reparameterization.} The current reparameterization check
is an artifact-level stability check over the evaluated transform. In the
current implementation, the array-level transform is
\code{basis_coordinate_reversal_v1}; a registry-only identity fallback is not
promoted as evidence for nontrivial reparameterization stability. This is a
bounded check, not a claim of full coordinate invariance.

\textbf{Source-facing queue.} A source-facing queue joins selected Gate12B
candidate rows back to prompt, answer, support-anchor, and conflict-anchor
text-surface rows. The queue is an inspection surface, not a new model run.

\textbf{Source-facing annotation.} Source-facing annotation is derived from
the queue by exact-anchor and phrase rules. It assigns
\code{support-following}, \code{conflict-following}, \code{non-gluing}, or
\code{ambiguous}. It is not human semantic annotation, not an answer-quality
label, and not a model benchmark.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Inputs}

Gate12B reads existing Gate12A registries:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{manifest.json}
    \item \code{explicit_triangle_cycle_registry.jsonl}
    \item \code{triangle_holonomy_registry.jsonl}
    \item \code{transport_relation_registry.jsonl}
\end{itemize}

When source-facing evidence is needed, the source inspection queue also reads
matching Gate12A triangle text-surface artifacts:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{triangle_text_surface_joined.jsonl}
    \item text-surface \code{manifest.json}
\end{itemize}

The queue builder requires the Gate12B run and the text-surface audit to share
the same \code{source_gate12a_run_id}. This prevents a candidate surface from
being silently joined to text rows from a different Gate12A source artifact.

\subsection{Observer Axis}

The default observer mode set is \code{core_v1}. It contains the core Gate12B
observer views used by the initial runner:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{all_edges}
    \item \code{anchor_qualified}
    \item \code{residual_chord_heavy}
    \item \code{relation_kind_conditioned}
\end{itemize}

The motif sensitivity uses \code{cycle_motif_expansion_v1}. This mode set
keeps the core views and adds ordered cycle-motif views:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{residual_first_leg}
    \item \code{residual_second_leg}
    \item \code{residual_third_leg}
\end{itemize}

The expansion is explicit. It is not a silent replacement for \code{core_v1}.
Its role is to test whether the archive relation-signature result can survive
strict observer support once ordered cycle-motif views are counted as separate
observer scopes where their cycle memberships differ.

\subsection{Scale Axis}

The current scale modes are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{triangle}
    \item \code{relation_kind_band}
    \item \code{anchor_policy_band}
    \item \code{residual_quantile_band}
\end{itemize}

Scale support is counted across the scale modes in which a cycle appears in
the top-k flat or high-tension surface for an observer scope. Candidate rows
must meet \code{min_scale_support}; they also must have at least one
non-triangle coarse-scale support mode. This prevents a row from being promoted
only because it appears at triangle resolution.

\subsection{Candidate Construction}

Candidate construction has three key constraints. First, the row must be
selected on a candidate side: flat or high-tension. Second, it must meet
independent observer-scope support. Third, it must meet scale support and
include at least one coarse-scale mode. Observer views with identical cycle
membership are reported together but counted as one observer scope.

The representative strict motif setting used for the paper tables is:

\begin{quote}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\code{observer_mode_set} & \code{cycle_motif_expansion_v1} \\
\code{top_k} & \code{3} \\
\code{min_observer_support} & \code{3} \\
\code{min_scale_support} & \code{3} \\
\code{per_band_limit} & \code{2} for source queues \\
\end{tabular}
\end{quote}

These candidates are candidate signals only. They do not classify answers as
right or wrong, and they do not score models.

\subsection{Bounded Local Reparameterization Check}

Gate12B records an artifact-level reparameterization check when array-level
transport operators are available. The checked transform is
\code{basis_coordinate_reversal_v1}. The check is stated in terms of
projector-level and transport-level compatibility under that evaluated
transform. It is intentionally narrower than any full coordinate-system claim.

If the array-level transport operator artifact is absent, the runner records a
registry-only identity path rather than promoting a nontrivial stability claim.
The paper therefore treats reparameterization sensitivity as bounded to the
evaluated artifact transform.

\subsection{Source-Facing Queue and Annotation}

The source queue returns selected Gate12B candidates to Gate12A text surfaces.
Each queued row carries candidate metadata, relation paths, prompt text,
answer text, support-anchor text, and conflict-anchor text. The queue builder
uses a bounded \code{per_band_limit=2} for the selected paper-facing archive,
transcript, and briefing source queues.

The derived annotation rule is:

\begin{itemize}
    \item exact conflict-anchor text in the answer gives \code{conflict-following}
    \item exact support-anchor text in the answer gives \code{support-following}
    \item no-gluing or not-warranted phrasing gives \code{non-gluing}
    \item otherwise the row is \code{ambiguous}
\end{itemize}

This rule is useful for source-facing grounding because it records whether the
answer surface follows the support anchor, follows the conflict anchor, or
declines a direct source merge. It is not human semantic annotation, not an
answer-quality label, not a model benchmark, and not a general evaluator.

\subsection{Strict Scale Support}

Archive strict scale support preserves the relation-signature flip at
\code{top_k=1}, \code{top_k=3}, and \code{top_k=5} under
\code{min_observer_support=2} and \code{min_scale_support=3}. The stricter
scale-support path weakens flat-candidate dominance relative to the baseline
archive run, but it does not erase the relation-side alignment: high-tension
remains R and flat remains M across the current dense-transformer archive
surfaces.

This is why the result is described as a relation-signature flip rather than
as a flat-dominance claim. The stronger statement is not earned by all
strict-support settings.

\subsection{Observer-Scope Sensitivity}

Strict observer support exposes an important boundary. With the default
\code{core_v1} observer set, raising observer support to the stricter setting
can make archive candidates vanish. This is not treated as a failure of the
archive relation pattern. It says that the default core observer set is too
narrow to support the stricter archive candidate definition.

The explicit \code{cycle_motif_expansion_v1} sensitivity restores strict
observer and scale support for the archive line. Under this mode set, archive
keeps high R and flat M across \code{top_k=1}, \code{top_k=3}, and
\code{top_k=5}. The expansion remains a sensitivity setting, not a new default.

\subsection{Motif Observer Specificity}

The motif observer expansion does not force every family into the archive
direction. Across the current 36 motif Gate12B runs, archive preserves the
high-R / flat-M direction under the strict motif setting, while transcript and
briefing mostly show the reverse relation-signature direction in the selected
source-facing queues. This matters because it makes the archive result harder
to explain as a trivial artifact of adding motif observers.

\subsection{Non-Archive Source-Facing Sensitivity}
\label{sec:nonarchive}

The non-archive sensitivity is a defense against a simple annotation-rule
artifact explanation. If the exact-anchor and phrase-rule annotation path
forced every family into the archive pattern, transcript and briefing would
also show the same clean high-R conflict / flat-M support-or-non-gluing
alignment.

They do not.

Transcript has 16 annotated rows: 10 \code{support-following}, 6
\code{conflict-following}, 0 \code{non-gluing}, and 0 \code{ambiguous}. Its
selected high side is dominated by M in 6/8 rows, while its selected flat side
is dominated by R in 6/8 rows.

Briefing also has 16 annotated rows: 12 \code{support-following}, 4
\code{conflict-following}, 0 \code{non-gluing}, and 0 \code{ambiguous}. Its
selected high side is dominated by M in 6/8 rows, while its selected flat side
is dominated by R in 6/8 rows.

This supports a bounded family-specific reading. It does not imply that
transcript or briefing are incorrect, noisy, or scientifically unimportant.
They are sensitivity surfaces that constrain the interpretation of the archive
result.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

The main threats to validity are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Existing artifacts only.} The study uses existing local
    artifacts rather than rerunning model inference.
    \item \textbf{Current dense-transformer set only.} The current evidence
    set contains four model lines.
    \item \textbf{Three rendering families only.} The family comparison covers
    \code{archive_v1}, \code{transcript_v1}, and \code{briefing_v1}.
    \item \textbf{Selected source queues.} The current source-facing queues use
    \code{per_band_limit=2}.
    \item \textbf{Derived annotation.} Source-facing annotation is
    exact-anchor / phrase-rule derived, not human semantic annotation.
    \item \textbf{Anchor dependence.} Source-facing annotation depends on the
    available support-anchor and conflict-anchor text in the Gate12A
    text-surface artifact.
    \item \textbf{Candidate definitions.} Candidate extraction depends on the
    current observer and scale definitions.
    \item \textbf{Motif expansion boundary.} \code{cycle_motif_expansion_v1} is
    an explicit sensitivity, not the default observer set.
    \item \textbf{Bounded reparameterization.} The reparameterization result is
    limited to the evaluated \code{basis_coordinate_reversal_v1} transform.
    \item \textbf{Packaging boundary.} Generated \code{runs/} artifacts are not
    yet packaged as a public release bundle.
    \item \textbf{No causal inference.} The artifact result does not imply a
    weight-level causal mechanism.
\end{itemize}

These limitations do not destroy the bounded claim because the claim is
deliberately artifact-bounded. The integrity checks and source-linkage checks
are recorded in the evidence manifest. The non-archive sensitivity tests a
simple annotation-rule-only explanation. The non-claims are explicit, and the

\section{Conclusion}

Gate12B reports a bounded archive-family closure-signature phenomenon in
existing LLM audit artifacts. The current dense-transformer archive surfaces
show a repeated high-R / flat-M relation signature that survives the recorded
observer, scale, motif observer-scope, and bounded local reparameterization
sensitivities. Selected source-facing rows align with that structural read:
high-side R rows are conflict-following in the selected archive queue, while
flat-side M rows are support-following or non-gluing.

The source-facing layer matters because it returns structural candidates to
the text surface without turning them into model-quality labels. The result is
therefore not just a relation-signature table, but also not a general answer
evaluator. It is a bounded bridge between a closure-signature candidate surface
and selected prompt/answer/anchor rows.

The boundaries remain central. The report does not claim a universal
interpretability law, a correctness classifier, a model-quality benchmark, a
physical invariant, a weight-level causal mechanism, or a checkpoint/release
result. Transcript and briefing sensitivities do not reproduce the same clean
archive alignment, which supports a family-specific reading while also keeping
the archive result bounded.

The next work is manuscript and release hygiene: table tightening, optional
expanded source-facing annotation, and intentional evidence packaging. It is
not the introduction of new observers before the current report is made
externally legible.
