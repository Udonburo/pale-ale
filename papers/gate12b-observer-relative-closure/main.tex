\begin{abstract}
We present Gate12B, a read-only secondary audit for studying
observer-relative closure signatures in already materialized Gate12A inference
transport artifacts. A closure signature is treated here as a relation-patterned
candidate surface, not as a correctness label or model-quality score. Across
four dense-transformer model lines, archive-family Gate12B surfaces repeatedly
place high-tension candidates on \code{residual_chord=3} and flat candidates on
\code{residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2}. The archive-family direction is
observed under the representative motif path and the scale-focused support
path. Under the core-observer boundary path, candidates can vanish, marking a
boundary of the default observer set. The artifact-level reparameterization
record is reported as a bounded availability-and-outcome record, not as
independent support. Under the selected source-facing queue reported in this
paper, high-side R candidates
align with \code{conflict-following} rows in 8/8 cases, and flat-side M
candidates align with \code{support-following} or \code{non-gluing} rows in
8/8 cases.
Transcript and briefing sensitivities do not reproduce the same clean archive
alignment. We present the result as a bounded artifact report, not as a
universal interpretability law, correctness classifier, model-quality
benchmark, weight-level causal claim, or physical invariant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM evaluation often compresses generated output into scalar scores or
task-level labels. Those views are useful, but they discard much of the
structure present in intermediate audit artifacts. This report takes a
different stance. It studies existing replay artifacts as structured records:
local objects, directed transport relations, explicit triangle cycles, and
source-facing text surfaces are inspected without rerunning model inference and
without converting the result into a model ranking.

The artifact line used here is Gate12A/Gate12B. Gate12A emits discrete
inference transport artifacts. Gate12B reads those artifacts as a secondary
audit and asks whether relation-patterned closure signatures remain visible
when the same candidate surface is viewed through different observer views,
coarse-grained scale modes, and a bounded local reparameterization check.
Gate12B does not alter Gate12A semantics, thresholds, classifications, or
source text. It is an artifact reader.

The Gate12B surface is downstream of the controlled structural replay regime
reported in~\cite{kawasaki2026structural} and the transport-first closure
formulation in~\cite{kawasaki2026telemetry}.

Informally, Gate12B asks whether the same triangle-level non-closure patterns
keep reappearing after changing which view of the artifact is used, how the
cycles are grouped, and how the local representation is rewritten within the
bounded transform available in the artifact package. The report therefore
studies persistence across artifact views, not free-form model behavior. The
object of interest is a candidate structural surface between raw generated text
and aggregate benchmark scores.

The main observation is an archive-family result. In the current
dense-transformer artifact set, archive-family Gate12B surfaces repeatedly
place high-tension candidates on \code{residual_chord=3} and flat candidates on
\code{residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2}. The same queue and source-facing
annotation path does not make transcript and briefing surfaces show the
same clean archive alignment. This contrast matters because it reduces the
risk that the archive result is merely a consequence of the annotation rule or
the motif observer expansion.

This report contributes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a read-only secondary audit framing for existing inference transport
    artifacts;
    \item an observer and scale sensitivity surface for closure signatures,
    together with a bounded artifact-level reparameterization
    availability-and-outcome record;
    \item a bounded archive-family relation-signature result across four
    dense-transformer model lines;
    \item source-facing queue and derived annotation evidence connecting
    selected structural candidates back to text surfaces;
    \item non-archive sensitivities showing that transcript and briefing do not
    reproduce the same clean archive alignment.
\end{enumerate}

The paper follows a deliberately conservative path.
Section~\ref{sec:positioning} positions the report relative to adjacent
interpretability and evaluation work. Section~\ref{sec:artifact-regime} defines the artifact regime.
Section~\ref{sec:definitions} gives operational definitions for external
readers. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the Gate12B read-only audit and
candidate construction. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the archive-family
relation-signature flip, sensitivity checks, source-facing annotation, and
non-archive sensitivity. Section~\ref{sec:why-matters} explains why the result
matters as an artifact-level audit signal. Section~\ref{sec:threats} states
threats to validity. Section~\ref{sec:manifest} maps claims to artifacts and
integrity checks. The conclusion restates the bounded claim and the non-claims.

\subsection{What We Claim}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bounded artifact phenomenon.} We report a repeated
    archive-family closure signature in existing Gate12A/Gate12B audit
    artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Archive-family relation signature.} Across four
    dense-transformer model lines, archive-family Gate12B surfaces repeatedly
    place high-tension candidates on \code{residual_chord=3} and flat candidates
    on \code{residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2}.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity boundary.} The archive-family direction is
    observed under the representative motif path and the scale-focused support
    path. The core-observer boundary path is reported as a setting where
    candidates can vanish, and the artifact-level reparameterization record is
    reported as a bounded availability-and-outcome record, not as independent
    support.
    \item \textbf{Source-facing alignment.} A selected archive source-facing
    queue aligns high-side R candidates with \code{conflict-following} rows and
    flat-side M candidates with \code{support-following} or \code{non-gluing}
    rows.
    \item \textbf{Non-archive sensitivity.} Transcript and briefing
    sensitivities do not reproduce the same clean archive alignment under the
    same queue and derived annotation path.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What We Do NOT Claim}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{No universal law.} We do not claim a universal law of LLM
    reasoning.
    \item \textbf{No benchmark ranking.} We do not present Gate12B as a
    model-quality benchmark or correctness classifier.
    \item \textbf{No answer-quality labels.} Source-facing tags describe the
    direction of a queued source row only; they are not answer-quality labels.
    \item \textbf{No weight-level claim.} We make no claim about model weights
    in general or about causal mechanisms inside a model.
    \item \textbf{No physical invariant.} The bounded reparameterization check
    is an artifact-level stability check over the evaluated transform, not a
    physical invariant.
    \item \textbf{No higher-order associator claim.} A later Gate12C-1
    negative-control extension tested compressed-overlap parenthesization
    sensitivity and returned \code{no_directional_support}; we therefore do not
    claim Type-III, nonassociative, or higher-order associator structure from
    that experiment.
    \item \textbf{No Gate12A checkpoint claim.} This report is not a Gate12A
    checkpoint or replacement for Gate12A.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Positioning}
\label{sec:positioning}

This report is adjacent to mechanistic interpretability, geometric deep
learning, and holistic model
evaluation~\cite{olsson2022induction,conmy2023automated,bronstein2021geometric,liang2022helm,lin2022truthfulqa},
but it does not attempt to localize circuits, probe hidden activations, or rank
models by task performance. It studies already materialized audit artifacts:
replay graphs, transport relations, triangle cycles, residual bands, and
source-facing text joins. The closest conceptual neighbors are structural
audit, replay analysis, and artifact-level evaluation rather than claims about
model-internal mechanisms.

The practical gap is between raw text inspection and aggregate scoring. Raw
generated text is inspectable but hard to compare reproducibly across artifact
views. Scalar scores are comparable but can erase the local transport and
closure structure recorded by the audit pipeline. Gate12B is positioned as a
middle audit layer: it preserves relation-patterned candidate surfaces long
enough for observer, scale, and source-facing sensitivity checks before any
human reader decides what the row means.

\section{Definitions and Terminology}
\label{sec:definitions}

This section fixes the vocabulary used in the report. The definitions are
operational. They describe how the artifact readers work; they are not
philosophical or semantic claims about reasoning in general.

\textbf{Gate12A artifact.} A Gate12A artifact is a materialized audit bundle
containing local proxy objects, directed transport relations, explicit triangle
cycle registries, closure residuals, and manifests. For source-facing
inspection, a matching Gate12A text-surface audit supplies prompt, answer,
support-anchor, and conflict-anchor fields joined to triangle-cycle rows.

\textbf{Gate12B secondary audit.} Gate12B is a read-only artifact reader over
Gate12A outputs. It builds observer-relative and coarse-grained views of
closure residuals, emits candidate rows that satisfy support requirements, and
checks a bounded local reparameterization condition when array-level transport
operators are present.

\textbf{Local object.} A local object is the artifact-level representation used
by Gate12A to attach replay state to nodes and transport relations. This report
does not interpret local objects as model-internal variables.

\textbf{Transport relation.} A transport relation is a directed artifact edge
connecting local objects in the replay graph. In the selected Gate12A surface,
relations can be summarized by relation kinds such as \code{trusted_tree} and
\code{residual_chord}.

\textbf{Trusted-tree edge.} A \code{trusted_tree} relation is an edge belonging
to the trusted-tree part of the selected Gate12A relation decomposition. It is
used as a relation kind in triangle signatures; it is not a correctness label.

\textbf{Residual-chord edge.} A \code{residual_chord} relation is a residual
edge in the selected Gate12A relation decomposition. It is a relation kind used
in cycle signatures; it is not an answer-quality label by itself.

\textbf{Triangle cycle.} A triangle cycle is an explicit three-leg cycle in the
Gate12A cycle registry. Gate12B uses these cycles as the base surface for
closure residuals and relation-signature grouping.

\textbf{Closure signature.} In this report, a closure signature is not a scalar
score and not a correctness judgment. It is a relation-patterned candidate
surface observed after applying Gate12B support requirements across observer
and scale views.

\textbf{Relation signature.} A relation signature summarizes the relation
kinds appearing on a selected triangle path. The shorthand
\code{residual_chord=3} means that all three legs in the selected triangle
signature are residual-chord relations. The shorthand
\code{residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2} means that one leg is a residual-chord
relation and two legs are trusted-tree relations. These strings should not be
overinterpreted as semantic labels.

\textbf{Candidate.} A Gate12B candidate is a cycle row that survives candidate
construction requirements. It is not simply a top-k triangle. A candidate must
have support across exact-deduplicated observer views, support across multiple
scale modes, and at least one non-triangle coarse-scale support mode.

\textbf{High-tension and flat.} High-tension and flat are residual-band sides
used by the candidate extractor. They are candidate sides, not correctness
classes. A high-tension row may be source-facing conflict-following, but that
does not make high-tension a general failure label. A flat row may be
source-facing support-following or non-gluing, but that does not make flat a
general correctness label.

\textbf{Observer.} An observer is an artifact-level view used to condition
which cycles participate in the candidate surface. Gate12B groups observer
views with identical cycle membership before counting support. Observer support
is an implementation-level count over distinct observer views after exact-set
deduplication, not a statistical independence claim. Observer views are merged
only when their selected \code{cycle_id} sets are exactly equal; highly
overlapping but non-identical views remain distinct in the current report.

\textbf{Scale and coarse-graining.} A scale mode groups the same triangle rows
at different levels of aggregation. The current runner includes triangle-level
scale plus relation-kind, anchor-policy, and residual-quantile scale modes.
Candidate rows must be supported across enough scale modes and must include at
least one non-triangle coarse-scale mode.

\textbf{Bounded local reparameterization.} The current reparameterization check
is an artifact-level stability check over the evaluated transform. In the
current implementation, the array-level transform is
\code{basis_coordinate_reversal_v1}; a registry-only identity fallback is not
promoted as evidence for nontrivial reparameterization stability. This is a
bounded check, not a claim of full coordinate invariance.

\textbf{Source-facing queue.} A source-facing queue joins selected Gate12B
candidate rows back to prompt, answer, support-anchor, and conflict-anchor
text-surface rows. The queue is an inspection surface, not a new model run.

\textbf{Source-facing annotation.} Source-facing annotation is derived from
the queue by exact-anchor and phrase rules. It assigns
\code{support-following}, \code{conflict-following}, \code{non-gluing}, or
\code{ambiguous}. It is not human semantic annotation, not an answer-quality
label, and not a model benchmark.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Inputs}

Gate12B reads existing Gate12A registries:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{manifest.json}
    \item \code{explicit_triangle_cycle_registry.jsonl}
    \item \code{triangle_holonomy_registry.jsonl}
    \item \code{transport_relation_registry.jsonl}
\end{itemize}

When source-facing evidence is needed, the source inspection queue also reads
matching Gate12A triangle text-surface artifacts:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{triangle_text_surface_joined.jsonl}
    \item text-surface \code{manifest.json}
\end{itemize}

The queue builder requires the Gate12B run and the text-surface audit to share
the same \breakcode{source_gate12a_run_id}. This prevents a candidate surface
from being silently joined to text rows from a different Gate12A source
artifact.

\subsection{Observer Axis}

The default observer mode set is \code{core_v1}. It contains the core Gate12B
observer views used by the initial runner:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{all_edges}
    \item \code{anchor_qualified}
    \item \code{residual_chord_heavy}
    \item \code{relation_kind_conditioned}
\end{itemize}

The motif sensitivity uses \code{cycle_motif_expansion_v1}. This mode set
keeps the core views and adds ordered cycle-motif views:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{residual_first_leg}
    \item \code{residual_second_leg}
    \item \code{residual_third_leg}
\end{itemize}

The expansion is explicit. It is not a silent replacement for \code{core_v1}.
Its role is to test whether the archive relation-signature result remains
visible under the representative motif path once ordered cycle-motif views are
counted as distinct observer views after exact-set deduplication where their
cycle memberships differ.

Observer support is counted after deduplicating observer views by cycle
membership. Two observer views are treated as the same exact-deduplicated
observer view when the set of selected \code{cycle_id} values is identical for
the candidate surface. This is exact set equality, not a similarity threshold.

\subsection{Scale Axis}

The current scale modes are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \code{triangle}
    \item \code{relation_kind_band}
    \item \code{anchor_policy_band}
    \item \code{residual_quantile_band}
\end{itemize}

Scale support is counted across the scale modes in which a cycle appears in
the top-k flat or high-tension surface for an exact-deduplicated observer view.
Candidate rows must meet \code{min_scale_support}; they also must have at least one
non-triangle coarse-scale support mode. This prevents a row from being promoted
only because it appears at triangle resolution.

\subsection{Residual-Band Construction}

Gate12B uses residual bands to define the two candidate sides used throughout
this report. The high-tension side is selected from the high-residual side of
the supported artifact surface, and the flat side is selected from the
low-residual side. These sides are computed after grouping rows by
exact-deduplicated observer view and scale mode; they are not semantic classes
and not answer-quality
labels.

The paper therefore uses \emph{high-tension} and \emph{flat} only as
extractor-side residual-band names. A high-tension candidate is not by itself a
failed answer, and a flat candidate is not by itself a correct answer. The
source-facing annotation layer later asks how selected candidates read when
returned to prompt, answer, support-anchor, and conflict-anchor surfaces.

\subsection{Candidate Construction}

Candidate construction has three key constraints. First, the row must be
selected on a candidate side: flat or high-tension. Second, it must meet
exact-deduplicated observer-view support. Third, it must meet scale support and
include at least one coarse-scale mode. Observer views with identical cycle
membership are reported together but counted as one exact-deduplicated observer
view.

\subsection{Candidate Extraction Summary}

The candidate extraction path is:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item read the Gate12A triangle, holonomy, and transport registries;
    \item construct observer views from the selected observer mode set;
    \item merge observer views with identical selected \code{cycle_id} sets into
    one exact-deduplicated observer view;
    \item evaluate flat and high-tension top-k membership within each
    exact-deduplicated observer view and scale mode;
    \item keep rows meeting the observer and scale support thresholds, including
    at least one non-triangle coarse-scale support mode;
    \item split retained rows into the high-tension and flat residual-band
    sides used in the reported tables;
    \item optionally join selected rows to source-facing prompt, answer,
    support-anchor, and conflict-anchor text surfaces.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Bounded Local Reparameterization Check}

Gate12B records an artifact-level reparameterization check when array-level
transport operators are available. The checked transform is
\code{basis_coordinate_reversal_v1}. The check is stated in terms of
projector-level and transport-level compatibility under that evaluated
transform. It is intentionally narrower than any full coordinate-system claim.

If the array-level transport operator artifact is absent, the runner records a
registry-only identity path rather than promoting a nontrivial stability claim.
The paper therefore treats reparameterization sensitivity as bounded to the
evaluated artifact transform.

\subsection{Source-Facing Queue and Annotation}

The source queue returns selected Gate12B candidates to Gate12A text surfaces.
Each queued row carries candidate metadata, relation paths, prompt text,
answer text, support-anchor text, and conflict-anchor text. The queue builder
uses a bounded \code{per_band_limit=2} for the selected archive,
transcript, and briefing source queues.

The derived annotation procedure is deterministic. A case means one selected
source-queue build for a fixed model line, rendering family, and
\breakcode{source_gate12a_run_id}.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The source queue is built case by case. Within each case, candidates
    are grouped by candidate side and relation signature. The
    \code{per_band_limit=2} setting selects at most two rows per
    side/signature group after deterministic sorting by candidate side,
    relation signature, residual-side order, and \code{cycle_id}.
    \item The queue materializes \breakcode{answer_contains_support_anchor_text} and
    \breakcode{answer_contains_conflict_anchor_text} using the answer, support-anchor,
    and conflict-anchor fields already present in the queue row. Matching is
    case-insensitive, collapses whitespace, and requires a non-empty normalized
    anchor string to occur in the normalized answer string. No semantic
    paraphrase matching is used.
    \item If the conflict-anchor match flag is true, the row is tagged
    \code{conflict-following}.
    \item If no conflict-anchor match is present and the support-anchor match
    flag is true, the row is tagged \code{support-following}.
    \item If neither anchor match is present but the answer contains one of the
    recorded no-gluing / not-warranted phrase patterns, the row is tagged
    \code{non-gluing}.
    \item Otherwise the row is tagged \code{ambiguous}.
\end{enumerate}

The current annotation helper uses the following literal lower-cased phrase
patterns for the \code{non-gluing} rule:

\begin{quote}

This rule is useful for source-facing grounding because it records whether the

\section{Why This Matters}
\label{sec:why-matters}

The result suggests that some audit artifacts may contain reusable structural
signals that are neither scalar benchmark scores nor free-form human
judgments. Gate12B does not decide whether an answer is correct. Instead, it
asks whether relation-patterned non-closure surfaces remain visible after
changing recorded observer views, scale grouping, and the bounded artifact-level
representation available in the current package.

This creates a middle layer for audit tooling. Raw generated text can be too
unstructured to compare reproducibly, while aggregate scores can erase the
transport and closure structure present in replay artifacts. Gate12B keeps the
candidate surface structural, then returns selected rows to source-facing text
for inspection. The archive-family result is therefore useful not because it
solves interpretability in general, but because it demonstrates a bounded way
to connect persistent closure signatures back to source rows without turning
them into model-quality labels.

For practitioners, the value of this surface is not that it replaces existing
evaluation metrics. Its value is that it preserves a structured audit trace
that can be inspected before collapsing an artifact into a scalar score. A
model answer, a replay graph, and a source-facing text row can disagree in ways
that are hard to express as accuracy, pass/fail, or preference labels. Gate12B
keeps those disagreements visible as relation-patterned candidate surfaces.

This makes the result useful as an audit primitive. It can identify candidate
regions where closure behavior remains visible across recorded observer and
scale views, then return those regions to source-facing rows for inspection.
The archive result in this report is a bounded example of that primitive: a
repeated structural signature is observed, constrained by non-archive
sensitivities, and grounded back to selected text rows without becoming a model
ranking.

A concrete use case is post-hoc audit triage for a document-grounded LLM
workflow. A red-team analyst or evaluation engineer could use the Gate12B
surface to identify rows where a generated answer follows a conflict anchor,
declines to glue available evidence, or remains structurally visible across
recorded artifact views. The output would not decide correctness automatically;
it would prioritize which prompt/answer/source rows deserve human inspection
before the workflow is summarized as pass/fail, preference, or accuracy.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

The main threats to validity are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Existing artifacts only.} The study uses existing local
    artifacts rather than rerunning model inference.
    \item \textbf{Current dense-transformer set only.} The current evidence
    set contains four model lines.
    \item \textbf{Three rendering families only.} The family comparison covers
    \code{archive_v1}, \code{transcript_v1}, and \code{briefing_v1}.
    \item \textbf{Selected source queues.} The current source-facing queues use
    \code{per_band_limit=2}.
    \item \textbf{Derived annotation.} Source-facing annotation is
    exact-anchor / phrase-rule derived, not human semantic annotation.
    \item \textbf{Anchor dependence.} Source-facing annotation depends on the
    available support-anchor and conflict-anchor text in the Gate12A
    text-surface artifact.
    \item \textbf{Candidate definitions.} Candidate extraction depends on the
    current observer and scale definitions.
    \item \textbf{Motif expansion boundary.} \code{cycle_motif_expansion_v1} is
    an explicit sensitivity, not the default observer set.
    \item \textbf{Bounded reparameterization.} The reparameterization result is
    limited to the evaluated \code{basis_coordinate_reversal_v1} transform.
    \item \textbf{Packaging boundary.} Generated \code{runs/} artifacts are not
    committed into this paper source directory.
    \item \textbf{No causal inference.} The artifact result does not imply a
    weight-level causal mechanism.
\end{itemize}

The source-facing alignment is intentionally small. The selected archive queue
contains 16 rows, and the strongest high-side annotation statement is 8/8 rows.
This is sufficient for a bounded inspection result, but not for a statistical
generalization. Larger \code{per_band_limit} queues and supplied human
annotation are future validation work.

This report also does not claim a residual effect-size result. The primary
reported surface is the relation-signature direction and its sensitivity
behavior. Residual distribution summaries should be added in a later evidence
package before making any effect-size claim.

A later Gate12C-1 higher-order negative-control extension tested whether
compressed-overlap parenthesization sensitivity exceeded a matched
spectrum-preserving orientation null on the same canonical twelve-run surface.
It completed with \code{no_directional_support}: all 24 run/q endpoints were
informative and coverage-complete, and all support calls were false. This result
is included as a boundary against overclaiming. It does not invalidate the
Gate12B closure-signature claim, but it prevents us from claiming Type-III or
nonassociative structure from the higher-order probe.

These limitations do not destroy the bounded claim because the claim is
deliberately artifact-bounded. The integrity checks and source-linkage checks
are recorded in the evidence manifest. The non-archive sensitivity constrains a
narrow degenerate annotation-rule-only explanation. The non-claims are explicit,
and the paper does not ask the result to support a broader conclusion than the

\section{Conclusion}

Gate12B reports a bounded archive-family closure-signature phenomenon in
existing LLM audit artifacts. The current dense-transformer archive surfaces
show a repeated high-R / flat-M relation signature. The archive-family
direction is observed under the representative motif path and the scale-focused
support path, while the core-observer boundary path is reported as a setting
where candidates can vanish. The artifact-level reparameterization record is
reported as a bounded availability-and-outcome record, not as independent
support. Selected source-facing rows align with that structural
read: high-side R rows are conflict-following in the selected archive queue,
while flat-side M rows are support-following or non-gluing.

The source-facing layer matters because it returns structural candidates to
the text surface without turning them into model-quality labels. The result is
therefore not just a relation-signature table, but also not a general answer
evaluator. It is a bounded bridge between a closure-signature candidate surface
and selected prompt/answer/anchor rows.

The boundaries remain central. The report does not claim a universal
interpretability law, a correctness classifier, a model-quality benchmark, a
physical invariant, a weight-level causal mechanism, or a Gate12A checkpoint
result. Transcript and briefing sensitivities do not reproduce the same clean
archive alignment, which supports a family-specific reading while also keeping
the archive result bounded.

A later Gate12C-1 negative-control extension further narrows the claim
boundary: the higher-order compressed-overlap parenthesization probe completed
with \code{no_directional_support}, so this manuscript does not claim Type-III
or nonassociative structure.

Future work should assemble a public evidence package, add broader
source-facing annotation coverage, and test whether the archive-family
signature persists under additional model lines, rendering families, and
artifact transforms. We do not introduce those extensions here; the present
report closes on the bounded manifest-level evidence mapping above.
