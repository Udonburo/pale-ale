## Core Summary

We report a bounded read-only audit of existing LLM replay artifacts. Gate12B reports an archive-family observer-relative closure-signature pattern across four dense-transformer model lines, using already materialized local objects, transport relations, triangle cycles, observer/scale views, and selected source-facing rows. Gate12B does not rerun model inference, rank models, or classify answer correctness. Gate12C-1 serves as a predeclared higher-order negative control: across the canonical twelve Gate12A source-run surface, a compressed-overlap parenthesization probe returned `no_directional_support` under a matched spectrum-preserving orientation null. Gate12C-1 does not expand the empirical scope of Gate12B; it narrows the claim boundary by preventing a stronger associator-excess hypothesis from being reported as evidence. The result is therefore not a Type-III, nonassociative-physics, model-safety, model-quality, or correctness-classification claim. Its value is methodological: the audit stack can report bounded structural signals while rejecting attractive overextensions.

## One-Page External Summary

Current LLM evaluation often collapses model behavior into final-output scores, pass/fail labels, or human preference judgments. Those views can be useful, but they can also erase structure recorded inside intermediate audit artifacts. This work studies those artifacts directly.

The paper presents Gate12B, a read-only audit over existing Gate12A replay artifacts. Gate12B does not rerun model inference, alter model weights, change artifact thresholds, or score final answers. It reads local objects, directed transport relations, explicit triangle cycles, closure residuals, observer views, scale groupings, and selected source-facing rows as an artifact-level audit surface.

The main bounded result is an archive-family observer-relative closure-signature pattern. Across four dense-transformer model lines, archive-family Gate12B surfaces repeatedly place high-tension candidates on `residual_chord=3` and flat candidates on `residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2`. This is reported as a repeated artifact-level relation signature in the current archive-family surface. It is not presented as a universal law of LLM reasoning, a model-quality benchmark, or an answer-correctness classifier.

The audit also returns selected structural candidates to source-facing rows. In the selected archive queue, high-side `residual_chord=3` candidates align with `conflict-following` rows, while flat-side `residual_chord=1|trusted_tree=2` candidates align with `support-following` or `non-gluing` rows. These source-facing tags are not semantic truth labels or human quality judgments. They describe how selected prompt, answer, and anchor rows behave under the recorded artifact surface.

A key part of the paper is what it does not claim. After the Gate12B result, a stricter higher-order extension was implemented as Gate12C-1. Gate12C-1 tested whether compressed-overlap parenthesization sensitivity exceeded a matched spectrum-preserving orientation null across the canonical twelve Gate12A source-run surface. The result was `no_directional_support`: all 24 run/q endpoints were informative and coverage-complete, and all support calls were false.

This negative-control result does not add empirical support to Gate12B. It does something different: it narrows the claim discipline around Gate12B. It prevents a tempting higher-order associator-excess hypothesis from being converted into a positive claim when the predeclared null-controlled test did not support it.

The resulting posture is conservative. Gate12B remains the bounded positive artifact result: a source-facing, observer-relative closure-signature audit over existing artifacts. Gate12C-1 remains a negative-control boundary: it shows that the audit stack can reject a stronger higher-order hypothesis under a matched null.

Without predeclared nulls and claim boundaries, structural audit methods risk becoming pattern-finding machines. This paper therefore treats negative controls as part of the audit method itself.

## Negative Control in Plain Language

A negative control is a guardrail against overclaiming.

Gate12B reported a bounded structural pattern in existing LLM replay artifacts. That result raised a stronger question: perhaps there was also a higher-order associator signal, where the order of combining local artifact transports changes the resulting structure.

That stronger idea was tested by Gate12C-1.

Gate12C-1 measured compressed-overlap parenthesization sensitivity and compared the observed values against a matched null. The null preserved each edge's singular spectrum while randomizing orientation. In plain language, the test asked:

Are the real artifact cycles more parenthesization-sensitive than structurally matched randomized cycles?

Under the frozen protocol, the answer was no.

That result matters because it prevents overclaiming. Gate12C-1 is not a failed search for a dramatic signal. It is a successful negative control: it shows where the evidence stops.

The paper therefore reports Gate12B as the bounded positive artifact-level result and Gate12C-1 as the boundary that prevents a stronger Type-III or nonassociative-structure claim.
